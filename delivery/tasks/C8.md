# C8 — the Drives tab, drawn as a blueprint

**Directed by the owner on 2026-11-14** ("Jarvis style with blueprints, in our muted organic theme"),
and approved with "Go" after reviewing the concept canvas
(https://claude.ai/artifact/E3uLKYHnHadSGXu4bHbnpy). Band M, 500 lines, declared up front.

## Goal

The owner opens Estate Map → Drives and reads each drive the way an engineer reads an elevation
drawing: its capacity as a dimension line, what is used as a filled section of the drive's outline,
what is free, and a callout saying when it was last catalogued. A drive that is nearly full is drawn
in the warning colour. The tab sits on a faint drafting grid. Nothing on it moves.

## Why

The Drives tab shipped in U0 with markup and no styles: on the real T480 its seven user folders sit in
one squeezed row beside a sentence wrapped a word wide. The owner's memory notes say the estate UI
must be visual, and the concept they approved draws the same facts as blueprints.

## What to build

1. **Each drive is an elevation drawing**: an outline whose width is the capacity, a filled section for
   what is used, a dimension line above labelled with the capacity, and the used and free figures. Its
   accessible name states the same figures in words.
2. **Unknown is drawn as unknown**: a drive whose capacity or free space the OS did not give is drawn
   as a dashed outline with no fill and the word "unknown" — never a guessed bar.
3. **Nearly full is a warning**: at 90% used or more the drawing takes the danger token and says how full.
4. **Detached drives stay present, dimmed**: drawn the same, dimmed, and labelled not attached.
5. **The user folders read as a list**, one per line, Downloads first, with "in OneDrive" and "you added this" as marks.
6. **The drafting grid** is two new colour tokens in `colors.css` and a background on the Drives tab; a
   monospace token in `type.css` carries the drawing's annotations.
